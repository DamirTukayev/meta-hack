# Buildings: SAM 3.1 against OpenStreetMap footprints

Generated by `scripts/eval_buildings.py`. On 44 photos (1.5 km squares: the four bundled ones, the twelve of `eval/CITIES.md`, 28 more places) the building mask of SAM 3.1 and the delivery points derived from it were compared with the building footprints mapped in OpenStreetMap (© OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL).
OSM is a yardstick only: it is incomplete in some places and of another date, so a point on an unmapped house counts against SAM here. A point is on a building when it lies within 3 m of a footprint; footprints under 40 m² are left out (the product ignores them too).

| What | All photos | Photos with 300+ mapped buildings |
|---|---|---|
| Delivery points | 31910 | 29726 |
| Points on a mapped building | **82%** | **87%** |
| Mapped buildings that hold a point | **46%** of 40448 | **45%** of 40405 |
| Mask pixels on a footprint (precision) | 72% | 74% |
| Footprint pixels covered by the mask (recall) | 74% | 74% |

Per photo, the share of points on a building ran from 2% to 98% (median 86%); the table is in `eval/buildings.json`. 42 of the 44 photos have 300 or more mapped buildings; where OSM has fewer the yardstick is nearly empty (houston-harvey: 29 mapped against 886 points, houston-harvey-b: 14 mapped against 1298 points), so the "on a building" share there says little about SAM.
Cost of this run: about $0.00 on the Meta API (answers are cached, so a rerun is free). Limits: the yardstick has gaps and its own errors; one tile per place; a point on a mapped garage or shed over 40 m² counts as a building; houses in a row come out of SAM as one blob and are told apart by the shape, which is a heuristic.
