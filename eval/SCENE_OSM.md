# Zone accuracy against OpenStreetMap land use

Generated by `scripts/eval_scene_osm.py`. The scene prompt was run 3 times per version on 16 photos (the four bundled ones and the twelve squares of `eval/CITIES.md`, 1.5 km each) with Muse Spark 1.3.
The zones are compared with the land-use polygons mapped in OpenStreetMap (© OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL; a yardstick only: coarse, incomplete, of another date).
Only pixels that OSM labels as residential, industrial, commercial or park/forest count (on average 52% of a photo), because OSM says nothing about the rest. A pixel is right when the model's zone there has the same kind; no zone there counts as wrong.

| Version | Pixels right | Range over repeats | Bundled photos | Twelve squares | Macro F1 | Pixels the model labelled |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| v1 | 63% | 63%-64% | 70% | 61% | 0.69 | 82% |
| v2 | 62% | 61%-63% | 67% | 60% | 0.70 | 77% |
| v3 | 71% | 71%-72% | 75% | 70% | 0.72 | 94% |

A model that ignored the photo and gave the whole image one kind would score: residential 39%, industrial 10%, commercial 32%, park_forest 18%.

Cost: a fresh scene call is about $0.01-0.02; the three versions on the 16 photos, three repeats each, cost about $1.8 in all (answers are cached, so a rerun is free). Limits: OSM land use is drawn by hand with its own idea of a boundary; it labels only part of every photo; a mapped park next to houses is a judgement call; one tile per place.
