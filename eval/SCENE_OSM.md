# Zone accuracy against OpenStreetMap land use

Generated by `scripts/eval_scene_osm.py`. The scene prompt was run 3 times per version on 44 photos (the four bundled ones and the twelve squares of `eval/CITIES.md`, and 28 more places of `scripts/prewarm_areas.py`, 1.5 km each) with Muse Spark 1.3.
The zones are compared with the land-use polygons mapped in OpenStreetMap (© OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL; a yardstick only: coarse, incomplete, of another date).
Only pixels that OSM labels as residential, industrial, commercial or park/forest count (on average 44% of a photo), because OSM says nothing about the rest. A pixel is right when the model's zone there has the same kind; no zone there counts as wrong.

| Version | Pixels right | Range over repeats | Bundled photos | Other squares | Macro F1 | Pixels the model labelled |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| v1 | 59% | 59%-61% | 70% | 58% | 0.63 | 81% |
| v2 | 59% | 58%-59% | 67% | 58% | 0.63 | 77% |
| v3 | 66% | 65%-67% | 75% | 65% | 0.65 | 92% |

A model that ignored the photo and gave the whole image one kind would score: residential 28%, industrial 6%, commercial 44%, park_forest 22%.

Cost: a fresh scene call is about $0.01-0.02; the answers are cached, so a rerun is free; the cost of the run that wrote this table is in `eval/scene_osm.json`. Limits: OSM land use is drawn by hand with its own idea of a boundary; it labels only part of every photo; a mapped park next to houses is a judgement call; one tile per place.
